# Coaching Calls: clip selection prompt

The user's prompt for picking clips (given 2026-10-26). Use it on every coaching-call transcript.
House overrides: every clip is at least 60 seconds (this beats "20-60 seconds" below), and only the best
teaching and inspirational moments make it. Write the result to `outbox/<slug>/<slug>-clip-report.md`.

---

You are an expert short-form content editor and trading-content strategist.

Analyze this trading coaching call transcript and find the best conversations and moments to turn into short-form clips for TikTok, Reels, YouTube Shorts, and similar platforms.

The audience is traders who are struggling with things like:

- Losing trades
- Overtrading
- Revenge trading
- Fear of entering
- FOMO
- Taking profits too early
- Moving stop losses
- Breaking trading rules
- Inconsistency
- Psychology
- Risk management
- Strategy confusion
- Lack of confidence
- Not trusting their setup
- Drawdown
- Prop firm challenges
- Difficulty following their trading plan

MOST IMPORTANT RULE

Whenever possible, use the STUDENT'S QUESTION, PROBLEM, CONFESSION, OR FRUSTRATION as the hook.

The student should ideally be the person who creates the curiosity.

For example:

STUDENT:
"I keep getting stopped out, and then price immediately goes in my direction. What am I doing wrong?"

→ This is a stronger opening than starting with the coach explaining stop losses.

Or:

STUDENT:
"I know I'm supposed to follow my plan, but when I'm down for the day I just start taking random trades."

→ Then transition into the coach's response.

Look for conversations structured like:

STUDENT PROBLEM → COACH DIAGNOSIS → COACH INSIGHT → STUDENT REALIZATION/SOLUTION

Do NOT automatically start the clip with the coach.

If the student's question is weak or contains unnecessary context, suggest a tighter starting point using the student's exact words.

---

FIND THESE TYPES OF MOMENTS

1. Student problem → powerful coaching answer

Prioritize moments where the student describes a common trading struggle and the coach gives a particularly useful or surprising answer.

Examples:

- "Why do I keep..."
- "I don't understand why..."
- "I keep losing when..."
- "What should I do when..."
- "How do I stop..."
- "I feel like..."
- "My problem is..."
- "I noticed that..."
- "Every time I..."
- "I know my strategy works, but..."

These are especially valuable because viewers can immediately identify with the student.

2. Student asks a question that traders are afraid to ask

Look for questions that make the audience think:

"Wait, I have the same problem."

These can be extremely strong hooks.

3. Coach challenges the student's thinking

Look for moments where the coach says, in effect:

"You think the problem is X, but the actual problem is Y."

These are high-priority clips.

4. Trading misconceptions

Find moments where the coach corrects a common misconception about:

- Entries
- Stop losses
- Risk/reward
- Win rate
- Psychology
- Technical analysis
- Strategy
- Backtesting
- Discipline
- Consistency
- Prop firms
- Risk management

5. Emotional breakthroughs

Look for moments where the student realizes something about their trading behavior.

Especially:

Student: "Oh, so basically I'm..."

Coach: "Exactly."

These moments can create very strong clips.

6. Contrarian / uncomfortable truths

Find moments where the coach challenges common trading advice or tells the student something they probably don't want to hear.

Examples:

- The strategy isn't the problem.
- You don't need more setups.
- Your risk is too large.
- You're trading too much.
- Your psychology problem is actually a risk-management problem.
- You're trying to make money instead of executing your system.

Prioritize these if the conversation supports them naturally.

7. Highly practical lessons

Find moments where the coach gives a specific rule, framework, checklist, process, or decision-making method that a trader could immediately apply.

---

CLIP SELECTION RULES

Prefer clips that:

- Start with the student's actual problem/question
- Make sense without watching the entire coaching call
- Contain ONE clear idea
- Have a strong payoff
- Are useful to traders
- Create curiosity
- Have emotional or psychological tension
- Can generate comments or debate
- Can stand alone
- Ideally fit within 20–60 seconds

Do NOT select a clip simply because the coach said something motivational.

The best clips should make a trader think:

"That's exactly what I'm doing."

or

"I've never thought about it that way."

or

"I needed to hear this."

---

HOOK OPTIMIZATION

For each selected clip, determine whether the student's original question is already strong enough to use as the opening.

If it is:

KEEP THE STUDENT'S QUESTION AS THE HOOK.

If it is too long:

TIGHTEN IT USING ONLY WORDS ACTUALLY SPOKEN IN THE TRANSCRIPT.

Do not invent a question that the student never said.

If the student's question is weak but the coach has a strong statement, you may recommend starting with the coach — but only when the student's problem cannot create a stronger hook.

---

EDITOR'S PRIORITY

When choosing between two equally educational moments, choose the one with:

1. Stronger student problem
2. More relatable trader pain
3. More curiosity
4. More emotional tension
5. Stronger coach response
6. Better payoff
7. Less context required

The goal is not to make the coach look smart.

The goal is to make the viewer feel:

"This trader has the exact problem I have — I need to hear what the coach tells him."

Finally, identify 5 recurring trader problems from the coaching call that could potentially become a content series.
